A useful customer checklist

Before placing a call or arranging a visit, gather a few details:

  • The address where service is needed and the easiest way to describe the affected area.
  • What you noticed, when it began, and whether it happens all the time or only sometimes.
  • Any recent changes near the fixture, if you know of them.
  • Steps already taken, including whether a shutoff was used.
  • Questions about the proposed work, timing, and any terms you’re asked to accept.

Don’t guess at the source of a leak or take a fixture apart to make the explanation sound more technical. Stick to what you can see or hear. If a problem is worsening, water is spreading, or you’re concerned about handling it safely, call a plumber instead of continuing to experiment.
